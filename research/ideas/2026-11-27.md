# Ideas from GitHub, week of 2026-11-27
## 1. Sector exposure cap on the main account's short-term stock buys
- What: When a new S2 or S4 stock buy is planned at a close, add up the market value of the short-term stock positions already open or queued in the same GICS sector (the table `strategies.SECTOR`, 77 stocks); the new buy may only use the room left under 30% of total account value (size = min(today's size, 30% x account value - sector value)), and is skipped if the room is under the $20 minimum; ETFs (S3) and the long-term funds are exempt, nothing already held is ever sold, and the cap is read once per planned order, in today's ranking order.
- Source: https://github.com/QuantConnect/Lean file Algorithm.Framework/Risk/MaximumSectorExposureRiskManagementModel.cs (read; it scales every holding in a sector down pro-rata when the sector exceeds a limit, default 20% of portfolio value); no peer-reviewed paper found for a sector cap on a daily-bar stock strategy (searched), so the evidence is wide use plus our own result for the momentum cap.
- License: Apache License 2.0 (LICENSE file of QuantConnect/Lean, read); code reuse allowed with attribution, but the rule is 15 lines of our own code, so we would only credit the idea.
- Better than ours because: today the main account's only concentration limit is 25% per position (config positions.max_position_pct), so two or three S2 breakouts in one sector (chip stocks in a chip rally) can fill the whole 60% short-term bucket and fall together; the failed D1 and D2 tests and the momentum experiment showed that clusters in one theme drive our worst drops, and our own sector cap on the momentum experiment (idea 2026-11-23-5, max 3 of 10 per sector) cut the median worst drop by 1.74 points for 0.48 points of gain. Different from that idea: this is the main account, a dollar share of the account instead of a count of picks, and it limits size instead of skipping. Honest limits: evidence is wide use plus one own result, not a paper; it may rarely bind and then show 'identical' in most starts, which would fail the 60% rule; no conflict with an approved rule.
- Would change: src/tradeagent/risk.py (new sector_room function next to bucket_room), the entry-sizing path in src/tradeagent/papertrade.py (simulate_portfolio, where queued orders are sized) and the live planner that calls risk.size_position, config.yaml (new positions.sector_cap_pct: 0.30, off by default), tests.
- How it would be tested: Rule fixed before any run: cap 30% of account value per sector, a single value with no tuning; 36 quarterly starts, 24 months each, 40/60 split, all current rules, in both money settings used for the 97-symbol test ($300 + $250 a month, and $1,000 with no deposits); design starts in 2015-2023, check 2024-2025, 2026 reported last; pass only if in BOTH settings the capped account beats today's rules in at least 60% of starts, median gain is positive, and the median worst drop is no more than 1 point deeper; also report how many starts the cap changed at all.
- Verdict: can build
## 2. Deflated Sharpe check on exit-menu and variant choices (testing tool)
- What: Before an exit or rule variant picked as the best of N tried is sent to the account test, compute its Deflated Sharpe Ratio on the 2015-2023 per-trade R series (the Sharpe is the mean R divided by its standard deviation, with N set to the number of variants tried: 48 for S2 and 27 for S3 in the exit study) and flag it if DSR is below 0.95; it only reports and can only reject, never accept.
- Source: https://github.com/skfolio/skfolio file src/skfolio/measures/_measures.py (functions expected_max_sharpe_ratio and deflated_sharpe_ratio, read); paper: David H. Bailey and Marcos Lopez de Prado, 'The Deflated Sharpe Ratio: Correcting for Selection Bias, Backtest Overfitting and Non-Normality', Journal of Portfolio Management 40(5), 94-107, 2014 (confirmed on SSRN and the journal citation record); related: Bailey, Borwein, Lopez de Prado and Zhu, 'The Probability of Backtest Overfitting', Journal of Computational Finance 20(4), 2017.
- License: BSD 3-Clause (LICENSE file of skfolio, read); code reuse allowed with attribution, but the formula is about 20 lines and we would write our own with a credit line, adding no dependency.
- Better than ours because: every one of our checks uses one held-out slice (2024-2025), and the exit study picked the best of 48 and 27 combinations on 2015-2023, a case the paper is built for; the S3 candidate passed step 3.1 by +0.22R and then failed the account check (better in 50% of starts), so a cheaper earlier warning would have saved a test. Honest limit: the S2 candidate's design gain was large (+0.250R vs +0.140R) and DSR may well not flag it, in which case the tool adds nothing and we drop it; the 97-symbol change passed only just (61% and 64% of starts) and has no such check. No conflict with an approved rule; it makes decisions harder to approve, never easier.
- Would change: new research/dsr_check.py only (read-only use of setups/simulate.py trade lists), plus one line in docs/REPLAY_FINDINGS.md method notes if adopted; no change to trading code or config.
- How it would be tested: Fixed before running: re-run the exit-study menus (S2 48 combinations, S3 27, same data, same 100-trade minimum) and compute DSR of the design-period winner with N = 48 and 27; pass (the check is worth keeping) only if it flags both known failures, the S2 winner (stop 2 ATR, no target, 30 days) and the S3 winner (stop 2 ATR, target 3 ATR, 15 days), as DSR below 0.95; also print DSR for the live S2, S3 and S4 rules with N = 1 for information; if either known failure is not flagged, the idea is dropped.
- Verdict: can build
